The Monty Hall Problem

Setup: You're on a game show. There are 3 doors. Behind one is a car; behind the other two are goats. You pick a door, say door #1. The host, who knows what's behind the doors, opens another door, say #3, revealing a goat.

Question: Should you stick with your original choice or switch to the remaining unopened door?

Answer: You should SWITCH! Your probability of winning increases from 1/3 to 2/3.

Initially, you had a 1/3 chance of picking the car. The host's action of revealing a goat doesn't change your original odds. But now, the probability that the car is behind the remaining door is 2/3.

The Missing Dollar Riddle

Setup: Three friends go to a restaurant and order a meal that costs $30. They split it three ways and each pay $10. Later, the waiter realizes there's a $5 discount. He gives $5 back in $1 bills. The friends each take $1 back (total $3), and they give the waiter a $2 tip.

Question: Each friend paid $9 (originally $10 - $1 = $9). Three friends × $9 = $27, plus the $2 tip = $29. Where's the missing $1?

Answer: There is no missing dollar! This is a trick question based on incorrect arithmetic.

The $27 the friends spent includes the $2 tip they gave. So the correct calculation is: $27 (what they paid) = $25 (meal cost) + $2 (tip). The $3 they got back makes the total $30.

Knights and Knaves

Setup: You're in a land where Knights always tell the truth and Knaves always lie. You meet two people and ask one of them, "Are you a Knight?" He responds, "Yes."

Question: Can you determine if he's a Knight or a Knave?

Answer: You cannot determine which he is from this question alone.

If he's a Knight (truth-teller) and you ask "Are you a Knight?", he'd say yes (truth).

If he's a Knave (liar) and you ask "Are you a Knight?", he'd also say yes (because saying "no" would be the truth).

The Birthday Paradox

Setup: In a room with 23 people, what's the probability that at least two share the same birthday?

Question: Is it less than 10%, between 25-50%, or more than 50%?

Answer: More than 50% (actually ~50.7%)!

Most people intuitively think it should be much lower. The key insight is that we're looking at all possible pairs of people, not comparing to a specific date. With 23 people, there are 253 possible pairs.

River Crossing Puzzle

Setup: A farmer needs to cross a river with a wolf, a goat, and a cabbage. The boat can only hold the farmer plus one item. If left alone, the wolf eats the goat, and the goat eats the cabbage.

Question: How can the farmer get everything across safely?

Answer: The farmer should:

  1. Take the goat across first (leave wolf and cabbage)
  2. Return alone
  3. Take the wolf across, but bring the goat back
  4. Leave the goat, take the cabbage across
  5. Return alone
  6. Take the goat across again
